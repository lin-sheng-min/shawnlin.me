Mercado de San Miguel is especially inviting at night. The glass and iron building glows from within, and before stepping through the doors you can already see the market crowded with people moving from counter to counter.

## One counter at a time

The best way to experience the market is to keep the first order small. There are too many things competing for attention to commit too early. A few pinchos can quickly lead to tortilla, seafood, and something unexpected from the next display.

The seafood counters were the hardest to walk past. Oysters sat on ice beside mussels, scallops, prawns, and sea urchins, while nearby plates of octopus arrived warm with potatoes and paprika. It felt less like choosing one meal and more like building dinner one plate at a time.

## The counter that stopped me

Even after eating, the full seafood display was worth slowing down for. The variety, color, and careful arrangement made the counter feel like part menu and part still life. Nearby, another stall framed preserved fish and seafood beneath the market's warm lights.

<aside class="journal-note">
  <span>Worth knowing</span>
  <p>Expect a crowd and walk through once before ordering. The small portions are ideal for sharing, but they add up quickly. I would treat Mercado de San Miguel as a place to sample, wander, and keep discovering rather than a quiet sit-down dinner.</p>
</aside>
